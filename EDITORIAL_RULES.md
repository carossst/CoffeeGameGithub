# Brew or False — Editorial Rules

These rules are the source of truth for all quiz statements and explanations.

## Product principle

Every card must test a fact that can be judged **true or false without guessing the writer's preference**.

Fast reading matters, but factual precision wins over punch.

## Source priority

Use the strongest source available for the claim:

1. Official or primary coffee references (for example SCA, World Coffee Research, ICO, government or public-health agencies)
2. Peer-reviewed research
3. Museums, universities, archives, or recognized cultural institutions for history and culture
4. High-quality secondary reporting when no primary source is available

Brand marketing pages can document that brand's own products or history. They should not be used to establish broad coffee facts when an independent source exists.

## Binary-question standard

A statement must have one defensible answer in the scope in which it is written.

Avoid subjective or undefined claims such as:
- best
- better
- premium
- high quality
- strong
- weak
- healthier
- smoother

Those words are allowed only when the statement defines the comparison or the source defines the term.

Avoid universal words such as `always`, `never`, and `every` unless the evidence really supports a universal claim.

If a fact varies by recipe, country, café, bean, dose, serving size, or time period, state the scope.

## Coffee-specific precision

### Caffeine
Distinguish:
- concentration from total caffeine per serving
- bean species from roast level
- coffee dose from cup size
- extraction time from coffee-to-water ratio

Do not imply that one variable alone determines caffeine when several variables materially affect it.

### Brewing
Treat brew temperatures, ratios, grind settings, and drink recipes as standards or useful starting points when they are not universal rules.

Do not describe hot brewing water as literally "scorching" already-roasted coffee unless a source specifically supports that mechanism.

### Drinks
Recipes such as latte, cappuccino, flat white, cortado, and americano vary across cafés and countries. Use `typically`, `commonly`, or a named standard when appropriate.

### Specialty coffee
Use the SCA's current definition. Do not present the historical 80-point convention as the sole current definition of specialty coffee.

### Freshness and date labels
Keep food quality and food safety separate. A best-by or expiration-style date is not automatically a safety deadline.

### History and market facts
For dates, "firsts", rankings, market leadership, store counts, and named people:
- prefer primary records
- state the country or market when relevant
- include the time period when the claim can change

## Rewrite standard

Allowed:
- shorter sentence structure
- clearer word order
- removal of redundancy
- everyday wording
- converting a weak statement into a cleaner binary statement while preserving the sourced fact

Not allowed:
- turning a tendency into a universal rule
- turning correlation into causation
- replacing a measured result with a broader claim
- keeping a catchy explanation after the source no longer supports it
- using an outdated definition without identifying it as historical

## Explanation standard

Target: 2 to 3 short lines.

A strong explanation should:
1. give the correction or core fact immediately
2. explain the mechanism, distinction, or context
3. end with a useful takeaway only if it adds information

Do not add a slogan merely for rhythm.

## False-item standard

For items whose answer is `false`:
- prefer a false affirmative statement
- avoid double negatives
- avoid traps based mainly on grammar
- make the false premise identifiable after reading the explanation

## Difficulty

- **Easy**: common coffee knowledge or one simple distinction
- **Medium**: requires a less obvious fact or distinguishes two nearby concepts
- **Hard**: requires a precise historical, scientific, market, or technical fact

Difficulty must reflect knowledge required, not awkward wording.

## Validation before publication

Every release must verify:
- exactly 200 unique question IDs
- IDs 1 through 200 are all present
- every answer is boolean
- every statement and explanation is non-empty
- every item has exactly one difficulty tag: Easy, Medium, or Hard
- declared TRUE/FALSE totals match the actual content
- factual edits have been checked against the best available source

When a source or definition changes, update the question rather than defending old copy.
