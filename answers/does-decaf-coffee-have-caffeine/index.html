Does decaf coffee contain any caffeine?

Yes, a small amount. Decaf is not caffeine-free: USDA data puts brewed decaf at about 2 mg of caffeine in an 8-ounce cup, against about 95 mg for regular brewed coffee.

Last checked: 2026-11-17

What the USDA data says

USDA FoodData Central lists brewed decaffeinated coffee at 1 mg of caffeine per 100 g, and regular brewed coffee at 40 mg per 100 g. An 8-fluid-ounce cup weighs 237 g in the same database.

Worked out from those figures, a cup of decaf holds about 2.4 mg of caffeine and a cup of regular coffee about 95 mg. Decaf is therefore roughly 2.5 percent of the regular figure, small but not zero.

Decaf espresso too

The USDA also lists restaurant-prepared decaf espresso at 1 mg of caffeine per 100 g. With 29.6 g in a fluid ounce, a 1-ounce shot comes to about 0.3 mg.

Why players get this wrong

The word decaffeinated sounds absolute, and many people read it as zero. The USDA values show something different: most caffeine is gone, but a trace remains.

Limits of these figures

These are database averages worked out by simple multiplication. A real cup of decaf depends on the coffee, the decaffeination process and the size of the serving, and other published measurements can be higher or lower. If you must avoid caffeine entirely, ask your doctor rather than rely on this page.

Common questions

Does decaf coffee have caffeine?

Yes, a small amount. USDA FoodData Central lists brewed decaf at 1 mg of caffeine per 100 g, which is about 2.4 mg in an 8-ounce cup.

How much less caffeine is in decaf than in regular coffee?

Using USDA averages, about 2.4 mg per 8-ounce cup of decaf against about 95 mg for regular brewed coffee, so decaf is roughly 2.5 percent of the regular amount.

Is decaf espresso caffeine-free?

No. The USDA lists restaurant-prepared decaf espresso at 1 mg of caffeine per 100 g, about 0.3 mg in a 1-ounce shot.
