What is the daily caffeine limit for most healthy adults?

About 400 mg a day, not 200 mg. For most adults, the FDA has cited 400 milligrams a day, about two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee, as an amount not generally associated with negative effects.

Last checked: 2026-11-17

What the FDA says

The FDA's consumer page on caffeine states that for most adults 400 milligrams a day is an amount not generally associated with negative effects. It translates that into roughly two to three 12-fluid-ounce cups of coffee.

The FDA adds that a 2017 systematic review of health outcomes, by the Institute for the Advancement of Food and Nutrition Sciences, confirmed the safety of the 400 milligrams a day level for most adults.

A reference, not a personal target

The FDA is clear that there is wide variation in both how sensitive people are to the effects of caffeine and how fast they eliminate it from the body. It also notes that what counts as too much can vary with body weight, medications, medical conditions and individual sensitivity.

So 400 mg is a reference for most adults. It is not a goal to reach, and it does not guarantee that every adult will feel fine at that amount.

Why players get this wrong

A lower figure such as 200 mg sounds plausible, which makes it a common wrong answer. The FDA's daily figure for most adults is 400 mg.

What this page does not cover

This page covers the general adult figure only. It does not give advice for pregnancy, for children, or for people with medical conditions or medications that affect how they handle caffeine. For those situations, ask a doctor.
