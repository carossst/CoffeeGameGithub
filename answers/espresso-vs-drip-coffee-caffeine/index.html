Does espresso have more caffeine than a cup of drip coffee?

Usually not per serving. Espresso has far more caffeine per ounce, but a standard 8-ounce cup of drip coffee holds more in total than a single 1-ounce espresso shot.

Last checked: 2026-11-17

The USDA numbers

USDA FoodData Central lists brewed coffee prepared with tap water at 40 mg of caffeine per 100 g, and restaurant-prepared espresso at 212 mg per 100 g. The same database gives the weight of common portions: 1 fluid ounce is 29.6 g and an 8-fluid-ounce cup is 237 g.

Worked out from those figures, per fluid ounce that is about 12 mg for drip coffee and about 63 mg for espresso. Espresso is roughly five times more concentrated.

Per serving, the order flips

An 8-ounce cup of drip coffee comes to about 95 mg of caffeine. A single 1-ounce shot of espresso comes to about 63 mg, and a double shot (2 ounces) to about 125 mg.

So a single shot has less caffeine than a regular cup of drip, while a double shot has somewhat more. What you compare, per ounce or per serving, decides the answer.

Why players get this wrong

Espresso tastes stronger, so it feels like it should carry more caffeine. Taste intensity and total caffeine are different things: the cup size matters as much as the concentration.

Limits of these figures

These are USDA averages worked out by simple multiplication. A real cup depends on the coffee, the dose, the recipe and the size of the serving, so treat the numbers as a guide rather than a measurement of your own cup.

Common questions

Does espresso have more caffeine than drip coffee?

Per ounce, yes: about 63 mg for espresso against about 12 mg for drip, using USDA averages. Per typical serving, a single shot (about 63 mg) has less than an 8-ounce cup of drip (about 95 mg).

How much caffeine is in a double espresso?

About 125 mg for 2 fluid ounces, worked out from the USDA figure of 212 mg per 100 g. Real shots vary with the coffee and the recipe.

Is the taste of espresso a sign of more caffeine?

No. Taste intensity and caffeine content are different measurements. Total caffeine depends on concentration and on how much you drink.
